## Purpose

The hook interrupts scrolling. The first line earns attention.

## First-line test

The first line should do at least one of:

- Create curiosity
- Create tension
- Create relevance
- Create emotional entry

For serious short video, a first line passes only when it also opens a question the body later pays off. Prefer a fact-bound scene, confirmed line, concrete number, real choice, or direct utility. “This case taught me”, “the real problem is”, and other summary openings fail when a supported moment can do the job.

After the first line, require at least two further information advances for a story-reveal route. Each advance must change what the viewer knows, expects, or thinks will happen; restated background does not count. Title, cover, opening question, completion payoff, and CTA must remain about the same supportable promise.

## Public adaptation boundary

These editing checks do not establish a fact, improve a draft by themselves, or grant publication authority. Record an actual rewrite and its reason when a check identifies a problem; preserve original evidence and author confirmation. Do not create a witness line or near-quote from a style example.
