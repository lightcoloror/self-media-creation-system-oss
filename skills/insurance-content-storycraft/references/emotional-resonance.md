# Emotional Resonance

## Core principle

Emotion is a designed audience response to meaning and change, not an adjective added by the writer. Do not write “she was devastated” unless that state is confirmed and authorized. Show the observable situation, pressure, choice, cost, and result; let the audience perform part of the interpretation.

## Three engagement targets

- **Interest:** What changed, and why should I keep watching?
- **Identification:** Where does the audience recognize a human situation, constraint, or choice?
- **Immersion:** Does causal progression make the audience mentally follow the situation?

Short social content often only needs interest plus identification. Do not inflate every item into immersion.

## Build an emotional beat

Each emotional beat needs a real beat reference, an intended response, the appraisal that could reasonably produce it, an evidence mode, and fact IDs. Evidence modes are observable cue, confirmed quote, or author reaction. Write the cause of feeling, not an instruction to feel.

## Identification without false universality

Connect a specific case to an ordinary situation such as scattered documents, waiting, uncertainty, conflicting priorities, or the cost of delay. Mark the basis:

- `audience-evidence`: repeated questions or observed audience data;
- `author-observation`: the author's bounded experience;
- `agent-hypothesis`: a testable idea, never stated as “everyone feels this”.

Specific detail plus a shared human pattern creates recognition. Generic emotional labels do not.

## Tension tools

- **Suspense:** what will happen next? Use only after an immediate reason to watch.
- **Curiosity:** what caused the current situation? Useful for result-preview openings.
- **Surprise:** a prediction fails and the meaning must be revised; the body must explain it.
- **Relief:** uncertainty or disorder becomes more manageable; do not promise safety or recovery.

## Risk-proportional limits

For medical, insurance, financial, bereavement, or other high-risk content:

- use low or moderate intensity;
- never target the affected person with humor;
- prohibit fear escalation, shame, outrage targeting, trauma exploitation, and false suspense;
- do not use one vivid outcome to imply population frequency, treatment superiority, coverage certainty, or product suitability;
- follow the story with a short explanation and necessary boundary.

Narratives can improve attention and understanding, but they can also bias decisions by making one outcome unusually vivid. The integrity gate therefore remains independent from the attention gate.

## Resonance review

- Can the intended feeling be traced to a fact, action, quote, or author reaction?
- Would the scene still work if all emotion labels were removed?
- Is the audience bridge supported, observed, or merely a hypothesis?
- Does intensity exceed the evidence or sensitivity of the subject?
- Does the ending leave an aftertaste, or explain the moral twice?
- Is the story illustrating a judgment rather than replacing evidence for it?

For short video, also test whether emotion is carried by a visible pressure-bearing moment. Prefer “he had not gone for the recheck”, “the doctor still only discussed surgery”, or “the next payment was due before reimbursement arrived” over explanatory labels such as anxious, difficult, or helpless. If the emotional line can be removed without changing the viewer's understanding of the pressure, it is decoration rather than resonance.
